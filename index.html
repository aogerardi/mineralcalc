About

Royalty income math
shouldn't be a black box

When a landman calls with a lease offer, most mineral owners have no quick way to evaluate it. The bonus sounds like a real number — but is it good? What would the royalty actually be worth over the life of a well? Most people sign without knowing, because the math has always lived inside an operator's spreadsheet.

MineralCalc puts that math in your hands. Enter your acreage and lease terms, and the tool builds a full income model: projected monthly royalty income, a year-by-year decline schedule, a sensitivity table across price and royalty scenarios, and a direct comparison between the bonus offer and long-term production value.

Built for Oklahoma mineral owners, landmen, and O&G professionals — the math works the same anywhere in the US. Every formula is visible, every assumption is adjustable, and every calculation is yours to share.

Not a substitute for a licensed landman, petroleum engineer, or O&G attorney — but a solid foundation for better conversations and better decisions.

Royalty rates in Oklahoma vary by play, operator, and how much competition exists for your acreage.

General benchmarks for active Oklahoma plays (2025–2026):

  • 1/8 (12.5%) — Minimum statutory rate. Common in older leases and low-activity areas. Generally considered unfavorable for mineral owners in active plays.
  • 3/16 (18.75%) — Previously standard. Still common but increasingly considered below market in the SCOOP/STACK.
  • 1/5 (20%) — Emerging standard in competitive areas.
  • 1/4 (25%) — Strong rate. Achievable in active plays with multiple operators competing for acreage.
  • 3/8 (37.5%) or higher — Rare. Typically only in extremely competitive leasing situations or for large acreage positions with significant negotiating leverage.

Rule of thumb: if an operator is actively leasing your area, you likely have negotiating power. The difference between 1/8 and 1/4 royalty on a single well can be worth tens of thousands of dollars over the life of production.

FAQ

Common Questions

Royalty income = (daily production × wells × 365 days) × price per unit × your royalty decimal × (1 − tax rate). Your royalty decimal is (net mineral acres ÷ spacing unit acres) × your lease royalty rate. Severance and ad valorem taxes are deducted by the operator before your check is issued.

Net mineral acres reflect your actual ownership in the mineral estate after accounting for fractional interests. If you inherit half of an 80-acre tract's minerals, you own 40 NMA. NMA is the standard unit for valuing and trading mineral interests — active Oklahoma plays commonly trade between $1,000 and $15,000+ per NMA.

Your royalty decimal is your precise fractional share of a well's production value: (net mineral acres ÷ gross acres in the spacing unit) × your lease royalty rate. It appears on your division order. For example, 80 NMA in a 640-acre unit with a 3/16 royalty = 0.0234375.

Oklahoma's gross production tax is 5% for a well's first 36 months of production, then 7% thereafter (HB 1010xx, 2018). The operator deducts this from royalty payments before issuing your check. Stripper wells and certain enhanced recovery projects may qualify for reduced rates.

In active Oklahoma plays (2025–2026): 1/8 (12.5%) is the statutory minimum and generally below market; 3/16 (18.75%) is common but increasingly below market in the SCOOP/STACK; 1/5 (20%) is the emerging standard in competitive areas; 1/4 (25%) is a strong rate achievable where multiple operators compete for acreage.

A lease bonus is a one-time payment per net mineral acre; royalties pay for the life of production. Compare the bonus to projected royalty income over the lease's primary term — if a well is drilled, royalty income from a single horizontal well usually exceeds a typical bonus within the first year or two. If no well is ever drilled, the bonus is all you receive.

Glossary & Methodology

Royalty income is your share of gross production revenue — paid to you without deduction for drilling or operating costs (unless your lease contains post-production deduction clauses).

The core formula: Monthly Net Royalty = (Production/day × Wells × 365 days) × Price × Royalty Decimal × (1 − Tax Rate) ÷ 12 months

Your royalty decimal is the key number: it represents the exact fraction of all production value you receive. It combines your mineral ownership percentage (NMA ÷ Gross Acres) with your lease royalty rate. A royalty decimal of 0.03125 means you receive 3.125¢ of every dollar of gross well revenue.

Severance and ad valorem taxes are then deducted to arrive at your net royalty — the amount you actually receive on your royalty check.

Net Mineral Acres reflect your actual ownership in the mineral estate after accounting for fractional interests throughout the chain of title.

Example: You inherit 1/2 of your grandmother's 80-acre tract → you own 40 NMA. Even though the land surface is 80 acres, you receive royalties as if you owned only 40 acres of the minerals.

NMA is always ≤ the gross surface or spacing unit acreage. When evaluating mineral purchases, price per NMA (commonly $1,000–$15,000+/NMA in active plays like the SCOOP/STACK) is the standard valuation metric.

Your NMA is documented in your title opinion or mineral deed. If unknown, a licensed landman can calculate it from the chain of title using the Duhig Rule and other title doctrines.

Oklahoma levies a Gross Production Tax (GPT) on oil and gas at the wellhead, administered by the Oklahoma Tax Commission (OTC). The current structure was set by HB 1010xx (2018):

• New wells: 5% of gross value for the first 36 months of production
• After 36 months: 7% of gross value for the remaining life of the well

Before 2018, the incentive rate for new wells was 2% (and 1% before 2015) — the legislature raised it to 5% to fund teacher pay raises. Stripper wells and certain enhanced recovery projects may qualify for lower rates.

The operator deducts this from your royalty payment before issuing your check. Different states have different rates: Texas 4.6% oil / 7.5% gas; Wyoming 6% flat; North Dakota ~10% combined; Colorado 2–5%.

When Oklahoma is selected with the "new well" option checked, this calculator applies the tiered structure automatically: years 1–3 of the projection at 5%, years 4+ at 7%. For other states, your entered rate is applied uniformly across all years.

All wells decline in production over time. This tool offers two models, selectable under the decline rate slider.

Exponential decline (default): Production in Year N = IP × (1 − Annual Decline Rate)N−1 — a constant percentage decline each year. Simple, conservative, and easy to audit.

Hyperbolic decline (Arps): the industry-standard model for shale. Decline starts steep and flattens over time following q(t) = qi ÷ (1 + b·Di·t)1/b, where the b-factor (typically 0.8–1.4 for shale) controls how quickly decline flattens. Once the instantaneous decline flattens to your terminal rate, the model switches to exponential at that rate — the same convention used in reserve reports. Hyperbolic projects more tail production, so cumulative income and NPV run higher than exponential at the same year-1 decline.

Shale/horizontal wells in Oklahoma's SCOOP and STACK plays typically show steep early declines (40–70% Year 1) followed by a flatter "tail" phase lasting decades. A 25% annual average is a common conservative long-term assumption under the exponential model.

The Oklahoma Corporation Commission (OCC) publishes well production records at wellrecords.occ.ok.gov — use actual well history to calibrate your decline assumptions.

Your monthly royalty check comes with a detail stub (sometimes called a revenue statement). Understanding it lets you verify you're being paid correctly.

Key line items to find:

• Well name / API number — identifies which well the payment is for. Cross-reference with OCC records at wellrecords.occ.ok.gov to verify it's actually producing.
• Decimal interest — your precise ownership fraction. Should match your division order. Calculate independently: (NMA ÷ Gross Acres) × Royalty Rate. Any discrepancy means you may be underpaid.
• Gross production / Gross value — total production from the well and its price. Divide your payment by this to back-calculate your implied decimal and compare.
• Deductions — any post-production costs deducted before your royalty is calculated. Check against your lease language. "At the wellhead" leases should have none.
• Severance tax — should match the applicable state rate (Oklahoma: 5% for the well's first 36 months, 7% after). Operators sometimes apply the wrong rate.
• Interest code / Product code — identifies oil (01), gas (04), or NGLs (05). Separate payment lines for each product stream.

If your calculated decimal doesn't match what's on the stub, contact the operator's revenue accounting department in writing and request a corrected division order. You may be entitled to back-pay.

You don't need to guess at production numbers — real well data is publicly available.

OCC Well Records (primary source)
Go to wellrecords.occ.ok.gov. Search by section, township, and range (found on your deed or lease) or by API number if you have it. You'll find monthly production reports showing actual BOE/day output going back to the well's first production date.

From your royalty check stub
Your check stub lists the well name and volume of production attributed to your interest. Divide your gross royalty by your royalty decimal to back-calculate the well's gross production for that month.

FracFocus.org
Shows completion data (lateral length, proppant volume, fluid volume) for horizontally completed wells. Longer laterals and more proppant generally correlate with higher IP rates.

Operator investor presentations
Devon, Continental, Ovintiv, and other public operators publish quarterly earnings presentations with average IP rates by area. These are useful for setting expectations on new wells before production is reported.

If a well is new and has no production history, Oklahoma SCOOP/STACK horizontals typically start at 500–1,500 BOE/day. 500 is a reasonable conservative starting point.

Your spacing unit is the acreage block designated by the OCC for a specific well or group of wells. Your gross acres field should reflect this unit — not the total surface area of your tract.

From your division order
The division order sent by the operator before your first royalty check will state the well name, the spacing unit description (e.g. "Section 14, T12N, R5W"), and the total unit acreage.

From OCC spacing orders
At occ.ok.gov, search the OCC docket records for spacing orders in your section. The spacing order establishes the legal drilling unit and its acreage. Most Oklahoma SCOOP/STACK horizontal units are 640 acres (one full section).

From your lease
Your lease will describe the lands covered using section/township/range legal descriptions. If multiple sections are pooled into one unit, add the acreages together for gross acres.

Rule of thumb
Modern Oklahoma horizontal wells: 640-acre units are most common. Some operators use 1,280-acre (two-section) units for longer laterals. Older vertical wells may have 40–160 acre units.

This is an estimation tool only. Actual royalty income depends on many real-world factors not captured here:

• Post-production cost deductions (gathering, compression, processing) can reduce royalties 5–25% — depends on lease language
• Actual well decline differs from the assumed exponential model
• Commodity prices are volatile — 30%+ swings over 12 months are common
• Lease terms (including deduction clauses, shut-in provisions) vary widely
• Complex title, partial interests, and multiple operators create real-world complexity
• Shut-ins, mechanical failures, and regulatory holds affect production timing
• This tool does not account for lease bonuses, delay rentals, or income taxes

NMA — Net Mineral Acres
Your actual owned mineral acres after accounting for fractional interests throughout the chain of title.
NRI — Net Revenue Interest
The exact fraction of gross production revenue you receive. For royalty owners, NRI equals the royalty decimal.
WI — Working Interest
Ownership share that bears a proportionate cost of drilling and operations. Higher revenue potential but cost-bearing obligation.
RI — Royalty Interest
Ownership share that receives royalties free of drilling and operating costs. Mineral owners under a lease hold a royalty interest.
BOE — Barrels of Oil Equivalent
Unit equating 1 barrel of oil = 6 MCF of natural gas by energy content. Used to normalize mixed production streams.
MCF — Thousand Cubic Feet
Standard unit of natural gas volume. 1 MMCF = 1,000 MCF. Gas wells measured in MCF/day or MMCF/day.
Royalty Decimal
Your precise fractional share of production value. Formula: (NMA ÷ Gross Acres) × Royalty Rate. Appears on your division order.
Severance Tax
State tax on oil and gas production at the wellhead (Oklahoma: 5% for a well's first 36 months, then 7%). Deducted from royalty before payment is issued.
Ad Valorem Tax
County property tax assessed on production value, separate from severance tax. Varies significantly by county and state.
Lease Bonus
One-time upfront payment per NMA paid when you sign an O&G lease. Separate from and in addition to ongoing royalty income.
IP Rate — Initial Production
A well's production rate when it first comes online. Reported in BOE/day or MCF/day. The highest rate the well will produce — all production declines from this peak.
Division Order
A document from the operator confirming your ownership decimal and authorizing them to distribute royalties. Review carefully — errors in the decimal interest directly affect your check.
ORRI — Overriding Royalty Interest
A royalty interest carved out of the working interest, not the mineral estate. Expires when the lease expires. Common in landman and broker deals.
Pugh Clause
Lease provision that releases depths or acreage not held by production when the primary term expires. Protects mineral owners from perpetually locking up acreage on a single shallow well.
NPV — Net Present Value
The present-day value of a future stream of royalty payments, discounted at an assumed rate of return. Mineral buyers typically use 10–15% discount rates. Higher discount rate = lower NPV. This tool discounts each year's income at end-of-year — a conservative convention; buyers' mid-year models run ~5% higher at a 10% rate.
API Number
Unique 14-digit identifier assigned to every oil and gas well in the US by the American Petroleum Institute. Used to look up production records, completion reports, and permit history at the OCC.
Spacing Unit
The acreage tract designated by regulators for a single well or group of wells. Oklahoma horizontal units are typically 640 acres for SCOOP/STACK horizontals. Your NMA is measured against this unit.
